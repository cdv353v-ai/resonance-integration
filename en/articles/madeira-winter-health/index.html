A sweater and a linen shirt:
everything you need to pack for a winter in Madeira

October 22, 2026 · Olga and Mark Vdovskikh

We have lived in Madeira for more than twelve years and still discover the island anew every winter, so instead of a list of tips we would like to tell you what winter looks like here in the west, in Prazeres, six hundred metres above the sea.

How you know winter has come

We have lived in Madeira for more than twelve years, and it is only in the last three or so that we have grown used to the fact that the seasons do change here after all, only not as plainly as on the continent or in the northern countries, so that winter does not arrive with snow, and instead one morning you simply realise that summer is over. You realise it because you want to wrap yourself in a soft, fluffy sweater and hide from the wind, and then during the day you want to sit in the sun like a cat, offering it first one side and then the other.

In Prazeres, six hundred metres up, the wind and seventeen degrees find their way to your back easily enough, but go down from these “heavens” to the ocean in that same sweater and it becomes obvious that you should have swapped it long ago for a linen shirt, because on a sunny day it can be twenty-seven degrees down there and plenty of people are swimming. And even there, right by the water, a cool little breeze still runs down your back now and then, because winter on the island is real, whatever the thermometer says, and you look at the tourists in shorts and light T-shirts and honestly wonder whether they are not cold.

Sky and sea

Sunrises and sunsets at this time of year are something special: the sky turns pink and orange, the clouds, if there are any, catch fire with it, and the delight does not wear off with the years, it still makes the heart stand still and takes your breath away, all the more because no sunset is like another, just as the sea is never quite the sea it was yesterday. By the way, the people of Madeira call the ocean the sea, and we have picked up the habit too, because it does not matter exactly where you are, as long as there is you, there is the island and there is the sea.

The Sunday market

And on Sundays in Prazeres there is the farmers’ market, with vegetables that smell so fresh and look so good that you seem to be fed by the sight and smell of them alone. What sets it apart from the other markets on the island is that only seasonal vegetables and fruit are sold here, and that is exactly what makes it precious, and exactly why almost everyone from Calheta and Porto Moniz comes here every Sunday.

Fog, rain and a fire

Sometimes clouds suddenly roll in and the village sinks into fog, everything is transformed, and it turns out that the fog is not cold at all: it wraps everyone like a warm blanket and finds its way into the house through open windows and doors. The clouds are not always so magically warm, though, and more often they bring rain, and then it is time to light the fire and watch the flames while the weather rages outside. That is the beauty of our island: at six hundred metres it can be pouring with rain while down by the sea the sun is shining.

Levadas, fish and waves

That is why in winter we choose levadas in the south and west of the island, where it is less damp and wet. The old trail from Prazeres down to Paul do Mar is sometimes closed in winter, because after rain the stones turn slippery and the way down is not easy, let alone the way back up, so if it is open and you do decide to walk down, take a taxi back, or after that climb you may well end up on our treatment table.

If you are going to Paul do Mar for fresh fish, come closer to lunchtime, because the fishermen bring in their catch after eleven, though not every day, since in winter the sea can be unpredictably restless. The surfers make the most of it, but only the most experienced, because the waves at Paul do Mar and Jardim do Mar give themselves to very few. In Calheta, by the marina, the water is usually calmer, but even there it is worth watching the sea for a while before you swim.

Above the clouds

We love driving around the west of the island: you head up into the mountains, rise above the clouds and see how beautiful and green it is, and suddenly understand how small it really is beside the vast mass of water all around.

Everyone who comes to see us says the same thing first: “It’s so quiet here!” And it is true, the big flow of tourists and cars has not reached us yet, and although the west has begun to develop as well, it is doing so nothing like as fast as Funchal and Câmara de Lobos. There is a plus and a minus in that: there is no bustle, but the infrastructure is not what it is in Funchal either, and we honestly believe the quiet is worth it.

Why recovery comes more easily here

In contact with nature a person cannot help slowing down, the nervous system settles, and the longer you live in a place like this, the calmer you become, while the body recovers naturally and without hurry, simply because the rhythm of life around you is different, a rhythm you can take home with you and even decide to live by there. We have often seen how our clients change in this rhythm, and, more importantly, they say so themselves. That is why in winter, when people come for a month, we offer a programme of four sessions spread across that month, and between sessions the island, the sea and the quiet carry on the work.

Small miracles

Madeira is a remarkable place where everyone finds a small world of their own, as if it had been made just for them, and it has not stopped surprising us even after twelve years. Every trip here becomes a discovery: an ocean unlike yesterday’s, a new sunset and a new sunrise, a gorge deep in the island where there is life too, a waterfall you somehow missed last time, hydrangea bushes that have suddenly grown huge. You could talk about these small miracles endlessly, and they all begin to reveal themselves once you simply slow down and allow yourself to be, to live, to enjoy and to love.

Frequently asked questions
What is the weather like in Madeira in winter?
Mild, but different at different heights. In Funchal the average temperature is around 20 °C in December and 19 °C in January and February, with about five hours of sunshine a day in December and January and six in February. In Prazeres, at 600 metres, it can be around 17 °C with wind, while by the sea it is noticeably warmer on a sunny day. Rain is more frequent in winter, especially in the north and the mountains, and it often rains up high while the sun shines by the sea.
Can you swim in Madeira in winter?
Yes, the sea stays around 18 °C and on sunny days plenty of people swim. The sea can be restless in winter, so choose your moment by watching the water first. In the west the water is usually calmer in Calheta, by the marina.
Where can you eat fresh fish in West Madeira?
In the fishing villages of Paul do Mar and Jardim do Mar. Come closer to lunchtime: the catch usually comes in after 11:00, though not every day, because in winter the fishermen cannot always go out.
Can you walk from Prazeres to Paul do Mar in winter?
The PR19 Caminho Real trail is sometimes closed in winter because it becomes slippery after rain. When it is open you can walk down, but take a taxi back: the climb is steep and hard.
How long is the recovery programme?
The core format is four sessions over a month, spaced so the body keeps changing between them. If you are on the island for less than a month, an express programme over 10 to 15 days is possible: write to us and we will see whether it suits your condition.
