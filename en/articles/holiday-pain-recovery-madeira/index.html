For Tourists

Ruined the Holiday.
Body Pain in Madeira

14 May 2026 · Mark Vdovskikh

Levadas, padel, surfing. Then the back or the knee. Why this happens on holiday and how to work with the cause, not the symptom.

The Illusion of Readiness

Madeira has a remarkable quality: it awakens in a person a life that has long been dormant. The scenario is almost always the same. The first breakfast with a view of the endless ocean, a deep exhale — and something shifts inside. The tension of the city falls away, and it seems as though the reserves are limitless.

The very next day you are walking a levada. The day after, you pick up a padel racket. By the end of the week you find yourself in the ocean on a board — for the first time in years, perhaps for the first time ever.

In that moment you feel a genuine return to yourself. Movement at last, clean mountain air at last, the body occupied with something other than endless sitting in a chair. But behind this elation is a trap.

Levadas are deceptive: they are not a walk in the park. Rocky ground, a constant invisible gradient, and kilometres that imperceptibly wear down the reserves of unprepared joints. Padel demands sharp bursts of movement that have been absent from your life for months. The ocean engages every muscle at once.

And at some point the body says: enough. The back refuses to straighten, the knee begins counting every step, the shoulder announces itself with a sharp flash of pain. Nothing is broken. The body is simply being honest: it was not ready for this much freedom.

The Trap of Temporary Relief

When pain appears, most people take the standard route. A painkiller, a day in a sun lounger by the pool, a light massage at the hotel spa. Things ease a little, and a dangerous illusion is born: "got away with it".

You return to active rest through gritted teeth. By the end of the trip the pain is back, but you reassure yourself: "I'll sort it out when I get home." But at home there is no time to sort anything out. Work, daily life, the familiar rhythm — and before long the pain becomes familiar background noise you live with until the next holiday.

What matters to understand: you have done nothing "wrong". The error lies only in the interpretation of the signal. In such moments the body is not speaking about an unfortunate movement on the court — it is speaking about what has accumulated. About how you have lived in recent years: the position you sit in, how you breathe, which demands you have avoided.

Pain on holiday is not an unfortunate accident or bad luck. It is a symptom of deep imbalance that has surfaced. A tablet simply silences the alarm in a burning building. Massage relieves surface tension but does not change the architecture of the problem. To genuinely return to form, you need to stop fighting the symptom and begin a dialogue with the cause.
