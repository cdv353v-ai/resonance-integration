Everyone Was Talking About Cortisol. The Industry Quietly Moved to the Spine

For the new generation of travelers, caring for one's own nervous system has stopped being a privilege and become a condition of the life worth traveling for in the first place, and chiropractic remains the only tool that works with this system directly, rather than through its consequences.

20 July 2026 · Mark & Olga Vdovskikh

Every few years wellness finds a new obsession, and tracing that path through gut health, sleep, cortisol, and the vagus nerve reveals the same pattern each time: behind every wave, for all its apparent novelty, stood the same address, the nervous system, which runs through every joint, organ, and tissue in the body and quietly organizes everything a person does, thinks, and feels, including what they never had to think about on purpose. The difference today is that this care has become more precise and more demanding: people no longer ask simply for "less stress", they want to learn to switch on alertness exactly when the moment calls for it, and to genuinely return to a state of rest once the threat has long passed, instead of carrying tension for years after its cause is gone.

Stolen Name

Chiropractic emphasizes the importance of the nervous system and its role in maintaining proper function and symmetry in the body, and in this sense it has, from the start, addressed the cause rather than the symptom: by adjusting the body, a chiropractor increases mobility, extends functional longevity, and reduces pain, inflammation, and internal tension, rather than simply muting them. Working with the spine, a practitioner touches several interconnected layers of the body at once, the communication between brain and body, the mechanics of the joints themselves, the symmetry of muscular function, and the state of the tissue around overloaded areas, which is why a single session rarely feels like a conversation just about the back.

For a discipline of this depth, chiropractic remains strikingly underestimated, and the reason lies in its own history. In 1987, a U.S. federal court found that the American Medical Association had spent decades running an unlawful campaign to push the chiropractic profession out of the market, and the fact that chiropractic is still associated in the popular mind, at best, with a "pop" for back pain should be read not as a verdict on its actual capability, but as the lingering echo of that very campaign.

Two Body Languages. One Spine.

The autonomic nervous system speaks two languages at once. The sympathetic branch prepares the body for action, quickening the pulse and narrowing attention, while the parasympathetic branch slows the heart rate, softens the breath, and lets recovery take over, and most familiar stress tools can only speak one of these two languages: cold plunges and high-intensity training address the sympathetic branch, while breathwork and meditation calm through the parasympathetic. Chiropractic occupies a rare position as translator between the two, precisely because both branches run through different sections of the very same spine, and a practitioner working directly with that structure can direct the body's attention wherever it actually needs support that day, whether that means calming an overloaded stress response, or restoring the clarity and vitality the body is missing.

A Word That Explains a Lot

A subluxation, a partial misalignment of a joint, can cause pain and restricted movement; among its symptoms are swelling, pain, and instability in the affected area, and its cause can be an injury, a sudden movement, or a chronic overload accumulated over years. This is a concept specific to the chiropractic profession, and it explains a situation familiar to almost everyone: a body that hasn't faced any single obvious trauma can still hold the same tension in the neck, lower back, or shoulders for years, and it is usually this quiet accumulation, not one isolated event, that finally brings a person in for a session.

Frequently Asked Questions
Nervous system regulation, in plain terms
What is a subluxation in chiropractic terms?
A subluxation is a partial misalignment of a joint that can cause pain and restricted movement. Symptoms of a subluxation include swelling, pain, and instability in the affected area. Causes can vary: injury, sudden movement, or chronic strain.
What does a chiropractic adjustment involve in our practice?
At its core, it is the correction of imbalances in the bones, ligaments, and skeletal biomechanics, combined with a particular sensitivity in the hands, trained to read the position of your bones. Much of it draws on traditional Chinese medicine, and much on Korean hand and foot reflexology. There is also a heritage from sports medicine, used to address ligament strain and the effects of old injuries.
What is nervous system regulation?
The body's ability to move between sympathetic (fight-or-flight) and parasympathetic (rest-and-digest) states as circumstances change, and to return to baseline afterward rather than staying braced. A regulated nervous system adapts quickly and settles when the demand has passed.
Does chiropractic only help with back and neck pain?
Most people first seek chiropractic care for back or neck pain, but the discipline's underlying focus is the nervous system as a whole. Beyond pain relief, many people also report improvements in sleep, breathing depth, and overall ease in the body.
How does chiropractic affect the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous system?
The sympathetic and parasympathetic branches of the autonomic nervous system run through different sections of the spine. Because adjustments work directly on spinal joints and surrounding tissue, a session can be directed toward whichever branch needs more support that day.
